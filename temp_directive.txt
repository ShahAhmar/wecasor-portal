1. Final pressure test before build -out 
Before expanding the portal further, I want us to pressure test the product one more time so we do not keep 
polishing the wrong architecture.  
• The portal must stop behaving like a generic admin dashboard and start behaving like a study -centric 
research operations system.  
• Documents cannot remain globally loose. They must be linked to study, site, and when relevant subject.  

• Different users log in at different times and with different responsibilities, so the interface cannot be identical 
for everyone.  
• Offline -friendly workflows are important in African operating environments, but not every module needs 
offline capability. We should be intentional about what is cached and what remains online only.  
• The super admin should be able to see the full portfolio at a glance, but ordinary users should only see what 
is relevant to their role, study, country, and site.  
• The platform must make daily work easier for coordinators, monitors, country leads, and PIs; it must not just 
look attractive.

2. Strategic positioning: WeCASOR vs PIRES  
WeCASOR should remain the execution layer for active research operations. PIRES should remain the training 
and readiness layer. They should complement each other, but they should not be merged into a confusing 
single document repository or generic learning  portal.  
• PIRES prepares people through training, onboarding, certification, SOP familiarization, and simulated CRFs.  
• WeCASOR runs live studies, site operations, subject data, regulatory packs, queries, analytics, and audit 
evidence.  
• A PIRES -trained user can be granted role -based access into WeCASOR after meeting readiness 
requirements.  
• Study data and regulated operations should stay inside WeCASOR. Training materials and readiness content 
can be synced or linked from PIRES where helpful.  
 
Recommended separation of responsibilities: PIRES for readiness, WeCASOR for active study execution, and a dedicated 
governance layer to prove what happened.  
3. Required portal hierarchy and navigation logic  
The portal must be rebuilt mentally and visually around a strict hierarchy. Every record, document, alert, and 
action should know exactly where it belongs.  
• Study: protocol package, study status, sites, dashboards, exports, lock status, CRF library, study 
documents.  
• Site: site status, country and city, site team, training downloads, regulatory submissions, site performance.  
• Subject: screening and enrollment status, visit schedule, forms, queries, documents, audit trail, sync status.  
• Data: eCRFs, outcomes, deviations, queries, reminders, exports.  
• Documents: linked to study, site, or subject context; never just floating globally without context.  

 
Recommended primary navigation flow from login through study, site, subject, action modules, and evidence layer.

Context -sensitive navigation rule  
When a user is inside a specific study, the menu and page titles should become study -aware. The same 
principle applies inside a site and inside a subject. This avoids users getting lost in a generic portal.

5. Screen -by-screen UI blueprint  
Below is the recommended screen logic with copy -ready labels, widgets, and actions. The goal is to leave 
nothing in the dark.  
A. Global command dashboard (Super Admin)  
• Header cards: Active studies, active sites, enrolled subjects, open queries, overdue visits, pending regulatory 
documents, failed sync events.  
• Portfolio chart section: Enrollment by study, enrollment by country, query aging trend, document expiry 
heatmap.  
• Alerts ribbon: sites with expired approvals, studies nearing lock, devices with sync failures, subjects overdue 
for follow -up. 
• Quick actions: New study, assign role, send bulk reminder, download portfolio export, review lock 
candidates.  
• Document insight panel: Total study documents, site documents awaiting review, subject documents 
requiring verification, training packs available.

F. Subject registry and subject profile  
Every subject should live inside a clean subject registry with filters and a timeline -driven subject profile.  
• Registry filters: study, country, site, status, subject ID, visit due status, query status, sync state.  
• Registry columns: Subject ID, screening date, enrollment date, subject status, next visit due, open queries, 
last update, site.  
• Subject profile tabs: Summary, Visits, Forms, Queries, Documents, Audit, Sync history.  
• Timeline view: Screened → Eligible → Enrolled → Baseline → Discharge → 30 -day → 90 -day → 180 -day → 
Completed / Withdrawn / Deceased.  
Subject ID and site numbering logic

should come first in the subject ID.

General CRF behavior rules  
• Every CRF should display the study name, site code, subject ID, visit name, visit window, form status, and 
last updated stamp.  
• Every CRF should support Save Draft, Submit, Reopen with reason, and Audit Trail view.  
• Validation should distinguish between hard stops and warnings.  
• If a field change happens after submission, reason for change should be required.

9. Offline, sync, and African feasibility requirements  
The portal should be practical for African field realities without overcomplicating the first build.  
• Keep high -level admin functions online only: user management, study configuration, full analytics, heavy 
exports.  
• Allow selected offline -friendly functions: subject screening, enrollment, CRF draft entry, follow -up capture, 
limited attachment queue.  
• Show sync state clearly on each pending item: Pending, Synced, Failed, Conflict.  
• Use lightweight pages, compressed images, and simple tables so performance remains acceptable on weak 
connections.  
• Cache only what is needed; do not try to make the entire platform offline from day one.  
• If several devices may work offline, allocate subject ID ranges carefully by site and device.  
Minimum low -bandwidth design standards  
• Avoid oversized hero graphics inside work pages.  
• Prefer clean cards, compact tables, and clear action buttons.  
• Keep the most important queue visible above the fold.  
• Do not force users to open many nested pages for routine work.  
• Provide simple mobile and tablet responsiveness for coordinators who may not always work from a desktop.

11. Audit trail, data lock, and evidence layer  
This portal will not be trusted simply because it looks clean. It will be trusted because it can prove what 
happened.  
• Audit log should capture who changed what, when, where, and why.  
• Field -level audit should show old value, new value, timestamp, user, and reason for change after 
submission.  
• Data lock should support at least Open, Soft Lock, and Hard Lock.  
• Unlocks should be rare, permission -controlled, and fully audited.  
• Exports should include audit -ready logs for queries, documents, and data changes.
